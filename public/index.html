We ask different questions before we build the answer.

“How do we increase conversion?”

We also ask:

Where is confidence being lost?

/

“How do we improve adoption?”

We also ask:

What is the existing behaviour accomplishing that the new system isn’t?

/

“How should we redesign the journey?”

We also ask:

Which decision should become easier because of the redesign?

/

“How do we automate the process?”

We also ask:

Which parts benefit from automation, and which still depend on judgement?

/

“How should we communicate this?”

We also ask:

What must someone understand, believe or feel confident about before they act?

Behavioural science changes the questions.

Better questions can change the intervention.

Sometimes the right answer is smaller than the brief.

You may not need more leads.

The opportunity may sit after acquisition.

You may not need another platform.

The constraint may sit in the process around it.

You may not need more automation.

The workflow itself may need reconsidering first.

We size the intervention to the problem.

We look for the one that matters.

An intervention only mattersif something consequential moves.

  • Revenue.
  • Conversion.
  • Customer value.
  • Efficiency.
  • Adoption.
  • Decision quality.
  • Business performance.
